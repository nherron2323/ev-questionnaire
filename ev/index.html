    Your electrical service

    Your service size tells us how much power your home can safely use at once, and how much is left for an EV charger.

    Main electrical service size
    Find the main breaker
    1. Open the metal door on your main panel (usually in the basement, garage, or utility room). Don't remove the cover screws, just open the door.
    2. Look for the largest breaker, usually at the very top, often labeled "MAIN."
    3. The number stamped on its handle is your service size, like 100, 150, or 200.
    4. No main breaker in the panel? Check for a breaker or disconnect outside next to your meter. The number on it is your service size.
    5. Still unsure? Pick "Not sure" and add a panel photo in a later step. We'll figure it out.
    ⚠️ Never remove the panel cover or touch wiring. Opening the panel door is all you need.

    How do you want to charge?
    We install an industrial-grade EV receptacle (not a standard hardware-store outlet) on a GFCI-protected 50 A circuit, as the NEC requires. Standard 14-50 outlets can overheat under hours of continuous charging.

    What will it cost to charge?

    Enter your electric rate if you know it, then slide to your typical monthly miles.

    Your bill has two sections: Supply (the energy itself) and Delivery (the utility's wires and service). For the most accurate number:
    1. Find total supply charges and divide by the kWh used that month. That's your supply rate.
    2. Find total delivery charges, subtract the fixed monthly customer / basic service charge, and divide by kWh used. That's your delivery rate.
    3. Enter both in cents per kWh. Example: $54.00 supply ÷ 450 kWh = 12.0¢.
    If you're on a time-of-use or EV plan, enter your off-peak rate, since most charging happens overnight.
